A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Acton property

Record the date, the weather and which outdoor item stopped, then note whether other lights or outlets in the house and garage still work. Photograph the item and its surroundings from a safe distance without touching anything.

Ask whether the loss is limited to the damp location or reaches other parts of the property, and ask the contractor not to assume a cause before the assessment. Never open the cover of the affected equipment to look inside.

What should the assessment establish?

One piece of outdoor equipment at an Acton home no longer works. The contractor establishes whether the loss stays with that item or is shared with other circuits or buildings, and lists each thing checked. Parts that sat out of reach are recorded as unexamined. Where moisture is involved, the damp-rated parts examined are named, and so are any needing replacement. The report keeps the repair itself separate from any wider concern, and notes whether ESA notification applies. The contractor explains the identified cause in plain terms before any repair is approved.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a dead outdoor outlet a supply problem or a wiring problem?

It cannot be told from the outlet alone. If lights elsewhere in the house still work, that points away from a supply event, but a contractor should confirm. Do not test or open anything yourself.

What should I note about a damp-location device that keeps tripping?

Record how often it trips, what the weather was and what was plugged in. Leave the device alone after the first repeat, and do not reset it again; describe the pattern to the contractor instead.

Useful project details

  • Rooms or devices affected
  • Equipment operating at the time
