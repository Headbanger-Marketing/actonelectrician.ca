Before work begins

Work out where a problem starts before you call.

Some homes keep every circuit under one roof; others may add a detached garage or workshop fed from the house. In the second case, that feeder is one more place a fault can start, and lights dimming only there may relate to it.

Note whether the whole property lost power or only one building or room, and whether neighbours reported the same. Write down the time and what was running, and do not keep resetting a device that trips.

Who should I call first when the lights flicker or go out?

Start by noting whether neighbours also lost power, since a supply event is the utility's concern. If only your property is affected, a Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess the installation. Do not open the panel or keep resetting a tripped device.

Why does a detached garage change what an assessment covers?

A detached building may be fed by its own run from the house, so the assessment has a second path to review as well as the panel. Ask the contractor to state whether that run was examined.

Which quote is stronger when each one names a different cause?

Compare what each quote says was examined and what remains unknown. A quote that names a cause without describing an assessment is harder to weigh than one that sets out its diagnostic scope and limits.

What records help when a fault may be inside an outbuilding?

Gather earlier ESA paperwork, invoices for work on the outbuilding, and photos of any labels there. Also note who can unlock it and who may approve changes to its wiring.

Once I press send, who contacts me about the work?

Not this site: any contact comes from a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor. The site, meant for reading, reviews each enquiry received but has no role in visits, pricing or electrical work. That party separately confirms who it is and its licence, then settles scope, price and dates. Before agreeing to anything, verify that licence with ESA.
